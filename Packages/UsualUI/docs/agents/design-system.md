# UsualUI: design system

## Implemented today

`Theme.swift` centralizes colours: neutral surfaces, ink, border, selection and the agent palette. `window` sits behind `pane` and a reply sits on `bubble`.

**Glass for what floats, solid for what you read.** The developer chose the system's controls and Liquid Glass on Mac and iPhone (November 2026, after Grok's app), over the earlier solid floating surfaces. What floats over the scrolling conversation is glass: toolbar items (iOS groups a bar's buttons in one capsule), `.glass` buttons, and `.glassEffect` for custom floating shapes (the name capsule, the message box, the menus above it, the call bar), in a `GlassEffectContainer` where several sit together. Nothing floating gets a hand-made fill, hairline or shadow. The conversation scrolls under the bars (`safeAreaBar`), and the system's scroll edge effect keeps the bars readable: soft on iPhone, hard under the Mac's name, where text beside it stayed readable otherwise. Bubbles, avatars and cards stay custom and solid, so their text keeps its contrast. Buttons in content are `.bordered` (`.borderedProminent` for the primary action, `role: .destructive` for destructive ones). Only Send, and the call button in its spot, take the Bot's colour (`.glassProminent` tinted with `Contact.accent`, ink in a room); other controls keep the app's ink tint.

**Text.** Chat text (replies, your bubbles, quotes, list items) gets its typography from `messageFont()`: `messageTextSize` in the environment (15 pt on the Mac, 16 pt on iPhone by default, adjusted by the Chat text size setting and, on iPhone, Dynamic Type) with 4 pt line spacing, regular weight in both modes. Markdown headings are that size plus 5, 2 or 0. Markdown blocks sit 10 pt apart so paragraphs stay distinct from the line spacing. Don't add per-view `.lineSpacing`.

**Dynamic Type.** Sizes are set in points as tuned at the default text size, through `scaledFont(size:weight:design:)`, never `.font(.system(size:))`. On iPhone it scales the size the way the nearest text style grows (13 pt like footnote, 17 pt like body); on the Mac, which has no Dynamic Type, it returns the size unchanged. Where a `Font` value is needed (text runs), use `Font.scaled(_:for:)` with the view's `dynamicTypeSize`. `messageTextSize` is already scaled where `appPreferences()` sets it (the in-app Chat text size first, then Dynamic Type), as is `composerButtonSize`, so sizes derived from them keep `.font(.system(size:))`. Sizes beside text (avatars, an icon column, a badge) use `@ScaledMetric` with a cap. Glyphs in fixed circles stay fixed, and the chat's name pill is capped at `.xLarge`, as the system caps its bars. At accessibility sizes rows wrap rather than truncate: `lineLimit(1, accessibility: 3)`, and a row's trailing detail moves under its title.

**Colour of a chat.** `Contact.accent` is the Bot's picked or palette colour, passed through `readableUnderWhite`, because white text sits on it in your bubbles and in the voice and send buttons. `chatContact` carries the contact to views inside a chat. It's nil in a room, where there's no single colour, so those fall back to `Theme.userBubble` and `Theme.primary`.

**Shapes.** Replies are `AgentBubble`: 18 pt radius, with joined corners at 6 pt where a message from the same sender touches. `UpToWidth`, not `.frame(maxWidth:)`, keeps a bubble hugging short text, since `maxWidth` fills what it's offered. `MarkdownView` must not set a width of its own for the same reason. Anything that asks you for something uses `cardSurface()`. Keep labels and actionable buttons there; don't turn approval or question cards into decorative status text.

**Motion.** New items use `AnyTransition.rise` (your message) or `.settle` (everything else); a reply taking over from its streaming draft uses `.identity`, so it doesn't flicker. `ConversationScroll` animates only when `arrival` changes in a chat that has finished loading, otherwise opening a chat replays every message. Each chat and room's transcript is keyed with `.id(contact.id)`: item IDs restart at 0 in every chat, so without it a switch animates one chat's messages into the next. `WorkingRow`, `ShimmerText` and `Breathing` all stand still under Reduce Motion, and any new motion must too.

**Cards sit where they appeared.** In a chat, the cards that wait for you stay after the message that was newest when they came, and what you send while one waits comes after them, with the reply and "Thinking…". Pinned to the end, a tall card (Connect) pushed your message and the reply out of sight on iPhone. "Thinking…" stays hidden while a card has nothing after it: the agent is waiting for you then. Rooms keep their approvals at the end, under replies from the members that aren't waiting.

Native SwiftUI layout and NavigationSplitView provide platform adaptation.
