# UsualUI: design system

## Implemented today

`Theme.swift` centralizes colours: neutral surfaces, ink, border, selection and the agent palette. `window` sits behind `pane` and a reply sits on `bubble`.

**Glass for what floats, solid for what you read.** The developer chose the system's controls and Liquid Glass on Mac and iPhone (November 2026, after Grok's app), over the earlier solid floating surfaces. What floats over the scrolling conversation is glass: toolbar items (iOS groups a bar's buttons in one capsule), `.glass` buttons such as Jump to latest on iPhone, and `.glassEffect` for custom floating shapes (the name capsule, the Mac chat bar's Browser and Computer buttons, the message box, the menus above it, the call bar, the Mac profile panel's page tabs (`ProfileTabs`: a glass capsule with a grey pill under the open page, as tall as the header buttons, reaching the sections' card edges past the header's 10 pt inset, with 12 pt before the page's first section), the Mac sidebar's footer, which the list scrolls under: the connection in a capsule, then Plugins and Settings in circles, level with the message box in height and bottom margin. On the Mac the header's name capsule and round buttons, the message box on one line and the footer items share one height, `ThreadPane.barItemHeight` (36 pt), and the footer keeps the list's 10 pt side inset, not the box's 20 pt, so its glass lines up with the rows' selection highlight and the sidebar's top bar. The box and footer keep as far from the window's bottom edge as the header's controls from its top (`ThreadPane.barEdgeMargin`, 9 pt, in chats and rooms alike); the rail keeps only two glass circles, Expand Sidebar (when the window has room for the list) above Settings: no connection dot, Search or New, which stay in the menu bar with their shortcuts; the developer's call), in a `GlassEffectContainer` where several sit together. Nothing floating gets a hand-made fill, hairline or shadow, except two on the Mac that sit on text: Jump to latest, a thin arrow on a solid `Theme.bubble` circle with a hairline, as in Grok's app, because glass drew it as a small pale disc lost in the text under it; and Add to Chat over a selection, a `Theme.floating` capsule with a hairline and a soft shadow, because the message's words showed through glass and crossed the button's own. The Mac's `.glass` button style draws that pale disc in the window you're at, which is why the chat bar's round buttons take `.glassEffect` like the name beside them. The conversation scrolls under the bars (`safeAreaBar`), which have no fill or hairline of their own: on iPhone the system's soft scroll edge blurs and fades it, and `EdgeScrim` fades it into `Theme.pane` behind the status and navigation bars and behind the message box down past the home indicator, as Grok's phone app does (November 2026; the soft edge alone left text sharp under the clock and the home indicator). The bottom scrim starts at the box's bar, not above it, so it stays off Jump to latest; on the Mac too since November 2026 (the developer wanted no solid band, as in iMessage). The Mac's soft edge only fades, so the Mac header sits on a full-width strip of glass reaching the window's top edge, extended 24 pt below the bar and faded out with a gradient mask and tinted with `Theme.window` at 40% (`HeaderGlass`; plain glass read as a pale haze in dark mode, and 70% as too dark): the blur is strongest at the top and dissolves into the conversation with no rim, line or shadow. The sidebar's list scrolls under the same glass at its top (under the window buttons and toolbar), covering only the toolbar's band so the list at rest sits clear of it, and has the soft edge at top and bottom. Under the message box the box's own glass does the blurring. Bubbles, avatars and cards stay custom and solid, so their text keeps its contrast. Buttons in content are `.bordered` (`.borderedProminent` for the primary action, `role: .destructive` for destructive ones). Only Send, and the call button in its spot, take the Bot's colour (`.glassProminent` tinted with `Contact.accent`, ink in a room); the message box's other buttons (attach, the mic, Stop, the iPhone gauge) are bare icons in `Theme.secondaryInk` (`.bare`), as in Grok's app: a glass circle each read as a row of filled discs.

**Text.** Chat text (replies, your bubbles, quotes, list items) gets its typography from `messageFont()`: `messageTextSize` in the environment (15 pt on the Mac, 18 pt on iPhone by default, adjusted by the Chat text size setting and, on iPhone, Dynamic Type) with 4 pt line spacing on the Mac and 2.5 pt on iPhone, regular weight in both modes. Markdown headings are that size plus 5, 2 or 0. Markdown blocks sit 10 pt apart so paragraphs stay distinct from the line spacing. Don't add per-view `.lineSpacing`. Links are underlined everywhere so they read in any colour: `Theme.link` (the system's link blue) in replies, the bubble's own ink in yours, where blue wouldn't read on the grey. Bare addresses become links only when written as one (`https://`, `www.`, `name@host`): the detector would take `README.md` for a web site.

**Quiet type in dark mode.** Measured against Grok's Mac app in November 2026, the sizes already matched (15 pt replies, 13 pt previews) and what made Usual Bot read heavier was contrast and weight. So dark-mode `Theme.ink` is off-white (`0xF0F0F0`) and `secondaryInk` a mid grey (`0x9E9E9E`, still 4.5:1 or more on every surface, the glass message box included); don't raise them back to white. On the Mac the sidebar's names are 14 pt medium, its section titles 12 pt regular, the chat's name 14 pt medium, and the date between messages is regular. Inline code is 0.9 of the text's size in `Theme.codeInk` on `Theme.codeChip`, quieter than the words around it; the chip is a text background, square and unpadded, because a rounded one needs a text renderer. Light mode was not compared and keeps its inks.

**Dynamic Type.** Sizes are set in points as tuned at the default text size, through `scaledFont(size:weight:design:)`, never `.font(.system(size:))`. On iPhone it scales the size the way the nearest text style grows (13 pt like footnote, 17 pt like body); on the Mac, which has no Dynamic Type, it returns the size unchanged. Where a `Font` value is needed (text runs), use `Font.scaled(_:for:)` with the view's `dynamicTypeSize`. `messageTextSize` is already scaled where `appPreferences()` sets it (the in-app Chat text size first, then Dynamic Type), as is `composerButtonSize`, so sizes derived from them keep `.font(.system(size:))`. Sizes beside text (avatars, an icon column, a badge) use `@ScaledMetric` with a cap. Glyphs in fixed circles stay fixed, and the chat's name pill is capped at `.xLarge`, as the system caps its bars. At accessibility sizes rows wrap rather than truncate: `lineLimit(1, accessibility: 3)`, and a row's trailing detail moves under its title.

**Colour of a chat.** `Contact.accent` is the Bot's picked or palette colour, passed through `readableUnderWhite`, because white text sits on it in the voice and send buttons. `chatContact` carries the contact to views inside a chat. It's nil in a room, where there's no single colour, so those fall back to `Theme.primary`. Your bubbles are `Theme.userBubble` in chats too, not the Bot's colour (the developer's call, November 2026): white on a Bot's colour reaches only 4.5:1, against about 14:1 for a reply, and a long message became a bright block in a dark window that made the replies around it harder to read. Grok's app puts your messages in grey too.

**Shapes.** Replies are `AgentBubble`: 18 pt radius, with joined corners at 6 pt where a message from the same sender touches. `UpToWidth`, not `.frame(maxWidth:)`, keeps a bubble hugging short text, since `maxWidth` fills what it's offered. `MarkdownView` must not set a width of its own for the same reason. Anything that asks you for something uses `cardSurface()`: the system's `.fill.quaternary`, the fill for large areas that hold other controls, with no border or shadow because a card sits in the transcript rather than floating over it. Fields on a card are `.roundedBorder`. Keep labels and actionable buttons there; don't turn approval or question cards into decorative status text.

**The pointing hand over everything you can click (Mac),** as the developer asked in November 2026, so you can tell what does. Each window's root sets `.buttonStyle(.clickable(.automatic))` (`appPreferences()`); a button with its own style wraps it, `.clickable(.bordered)`; the app's own styles (`.inkProminent`, `.bare`, `.systemSwitch`) carry it; pickers and menus, which take no button style, get `.clickable()`. A new control needs the same, or it shows the arrow. Settings' tab bar and menu items are AppKit's and keep the arrow.

**Switches** are the system's colour, the Mac's accent (blue by default) and iPhone's green, through `.toggleStyle(.systemSwitch)`: the ink tint drew them near white in dark mode.

**Buttons.** Buttons in content are the system's: `.bordered`, `.borderedProminent` for the one main action, `role: .destructive` for anything that deletes, and `.borderless` for an icon alone, as the profile pages' New Routine, New App and New Item are (a bare plus in `ProfileSectionHeader`, at the developer's request; they were filled before). Every section title in the profile panel goes through `ProfileSectionHeader`, small and grey: the grouped form drew plain titles inconsistently, some small and grey, some large and bold. The main action is `.inkProminent` (`.inkGlassProminent` when it floats), never `.borderedProminent` directly: the system draws its label white, which disappears on the ink tint in dark mode, so on iPhone the label is `Theme.primaryInk`, and the Mac tints with `Theme.prominent`, a grey white text reads on in dark mode (a background window draws it unfilled in its label colour). Cards wrap that in `cardButton(prominent:fill:)`. Card buttons are regular on iPhone (large only stacked full width, as Forge's approval is) and large on the Mac. The message hover bar's buttons are bare icons too (`.bare`, `Theme.secondaryInk`), beside the time; reaction pills are small bordered capsules in the chat's colour. Kept custom on purpose: bubbles, avatars, the Bot's colour on Send, quiet transcript rows (a tool result, folded messages), and selectable rows (New Room's members, the app catalog, routine templates and runs), which keep `.clickable()`.

**Sheets.** Every sheet that asks for something is `SheetForm` around a `Form` (viewers such as a picture or Select Text are a `NavigationStack` with Done), which it groups and puts in a `NavigationStack` with its buttons as toolbar items: Cancel or Back at `.cancellationAction`, the action at `.confirmationAction`. On iPhone Cancel is the system ✕ and the action is `.glassProminent`, because the bar otherwise draws it plain and glass picks a label that reads on ink. On the Mac they sit at the bottom right with Esc and Return, except that an action Return mustn't press (`isDefault: false`, Delete Bot) takes `.destructiveAction`, at the bottom left, since the Mac may make a confirmation item the default button. Explanations and problems go in section footers. A sheet sets its Mac size with `frame` and its iPhone detents itself; don't give it a `presentationBackground`, which hides the grouped form's own.

**Motion.** New items use `AnyTransition.rise` (your message) or `.settle` (everything else); a reply taking over from its streaming draft uses `.identity`, so it doesn't flicker. `ConversationScroll` animates only when `arrival` changes in a chat that has finished loading, otherwise opening a chat replays every message. Each chat and room's transcript is keyed with `.id(contact.id)`: item IDs restart at 0 in every chat, so without it a switch animates one chat's messages into the next. `WorkingRow`, `ShimmerText` and `Breathing` all stand still under Reduce Motion, and any new motion must too.

**Cards sit where they appeared.** In a chat, the cards that wait for you stay after the message that was newest when they came, and what you send while one waits comes after them, with the reply and "Thinking…". Pinned to the end, a tall card (Connect) pushed your message and the reply out of sight on iPhone. "Thinking…" stays hidden while a card has nothing after it: the agent is waiting for you then. Rooms keep their approvals at the end, under replies from the members that aren't waiting.

**Shell, lists and settings use the system's components.** Toolbar buttons are plain toolbar items, which the system draws in glass; the iPhone chat list is a plain `List` under a large "Chats" title with `.searchable`; Settings, the profile pages, the routine detail and Connect are grouped `Form`s; Mac Settings is the `Settings` scene with a tab per page. What floats (the ⌥Space panel, the ⌘K palette, the search notice) is `.glassEffect`, and a field or row on that glass uses a `.fill` rather than more glass. A button inside a list row that also holds a switch or another button stays `.clickable` (plain), or a tap on the row runs them all. Still drawn by the app: the Mac sidebar's rows (a stack, so the selected row is a grey pill, sections take drops and the list folds to the rail), each chat row's content, avatars, section headings and the gateway status button.

Native SwiftUI layout and NavigationSplitView provide platform adaptation.
